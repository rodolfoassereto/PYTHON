1) TVW1 inpainting

This is an inpainting problem, where we are trying to reconstruct the central voxel (only). The fidelity is the characteristic function on all but the central voxel; the regularization is the (unbalanced and anisotropic) TVW1, i.e. the sum of the (unbalanced) W1 between neighboring voxels.
Since we are using anisotropic TV, we expect to recover exactly the W1 median of the content from the voxels in (0,1), (1,0), (1,2), (1,1). The result is a centered star, which coincides with the experiments in Enis and Carlier's paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01765 (pag.3).

2) Reconstructing gaussians

This is an (unbalanced) W1-TVW1 reconstruction. The data consists of gaussians oriented vertically, and rotated by a random angle. The TVW1 regularization straightens the gaussians back vertically. A "staircasing effect" on the angles seems to appear.

3) Reconstructing a curve

This is another (unbalanced) W1-TVW1 reconstruction with 1D domain, i.e. a curve in the space of measures.
The "reconstruction with TVW" image shows the given data on the left, and the effect of the (unbalanced) W1-TVW1 reconstruction with increasing regularization parameter. Mass gets transported and a regularization on the position appears.
The "reconstruction with TVL1" does the same, but for a W1-TVL1 reconstruction. Mass gets split.
The "reconstruction with L1-TVL1" does the same, but for an L1-TVL1 reconstruction. Mass gets teleported.



Remark: we are always using an unbalanced Wasserstein (the Hanin norm or Piccoli-Rossi distance, which are the same) allowing for mass creation or destruction.
